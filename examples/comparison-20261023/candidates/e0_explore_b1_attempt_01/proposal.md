# Proximity propagation inside disjunctive layout search

I read both supplied proposals in full: baseline/proposal.md and attempt_00/proposal.md, together with all three supplied score snapshots and their source files. No additional historical proposals or failure logs are supplied.

The baseline and its identical init snapshot measured combined score 0.6738452380952381, support 1.0, collision freedom 0.9583333333333334, relation satisfaction 0.21666666666666667, and zero strict successes. Its code ignores relations and centers every child on its parent; these explain its ordering failures and recurring sibling collisions. Attempt_00 introduced difference constraints, collision branching, and proximity projection. It measured 1.0 combined score and strict success on all six development scenes. This is evidence of success on those scenes, not evidence about unseen configurations. There is no measured failed constraint-solver attempt to repair and no established plateau across repeated variants.

The new mechanism combines that successful graph representation with circular proximity propagation during search. All-pairs difference-constraint closure supplies a necessary minimum separation on each axis. A near disk then bounds the other axis by sqrt(radius squared minus minimum separation squared). These bounds are inserted back into the graphs and propagated again. Thus proximity can constrain packing choices before coordinates are projected, rather than acting only as a post-placement repair. This cross-axis propagation was absent from both historical implementations.

Search prioritizes states satisfying the propagated proximity constraints, branches on collisions with the fewest graph-feasible separating choices, and retains a bounded soft-relation recovery search for contradictory inputs. Dykstra projection remains available for the residual curved constraints. Candidate selection uses the supplied geometric objective and retains room-contained fallbacks.

Expected benefit is earlier rejection of incompatible packing directions and less projection work on narrow proximity constraints. Risks include cubic closure cost, bounded search missing a packing, and slow projection near tangencies. Propagation uses outward numerical slack so its linear bounds are conservative. Work is bounded and uses only the standard library. The submitted code has not been executed or evaluated; no improvement over the observed historical score is claimed.
